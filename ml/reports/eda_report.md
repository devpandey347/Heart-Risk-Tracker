## Key Observations from EDA

> ⚠ These are *statistical associations* in a specific 1988 hospital dataset. They are **not causal claims** and cannot be used for medical advice.

### Strongest signals (useful for prediction)

1. **`thalach` (max heart rate)** — Patients *with* disease tend to have a *lower* maximum heart rate. The medians differ by roughly 20 bpm. This is one of the strongest single-feature signals.

2. **`oldpeak` (ST depression)** — Patients *with* disease tend to have a *higher* ST depression value. A value of 0 is far more common in the "no disease" group.

3. **`cp` (chest pain type)** — Asymptomatic chest pain (`cp=4`) is strongly associated with disease presence, which is counter-intuitive but well-documented in this dataset. Typical angina (`cp=1`) has lower disease prevalence here.

4. **`exang` (exercise-induced angina)** — Patients who experience angina during exercise (`exang=1`) have much higher disease prevalence.

5. **`thal` (thalassemia type)** — Reversible defect (`thal=7`) is strongly associated with disease.

### Weaker signals

6. **`age`** — Older patients have slightly higher disease prevalence, but there is significant overlap between groups.

7. **`sex`** — Males (`sex=1`) show higher disease prevalence in this dataset, but the dataset is gender-imbalanced (mostly male patients), which limits generalisation.

8. **`chol` (cholesterol)** — Surprisingly weak separation between classes in this dataset. Cholesterol alone is a poor discriminator here.

### Correlation notes

- Pearson correlation is only reliable for numerical features measured on a continuous scale.
- For categorical features like `cp`, `thal`, `exang`, the appropriate measure would be **Cramér's V** or **point-biserial correlation** — these are computed during training-time feature selection, not in EDA.
- The highest Pearson correlation with the binary target is expected from `thalach` (negative) and `oldpeak` (positive).

## Dataset Limitations

> These limitations must be disclosed whenever presenting this project.

1. **Small sample**: 303 patients is a very small dataset by modern ML standards. Models trained on it will not generalise reliably to diverse real-world populations.

2. **Single hospital, single era**: Data collected in 1988 at the Cleveland Clinic Foundation. Medical practices, populations, and diagnostic criteria have changed.

3. **Demographic skew**: The dataset skews heavily male and toward middle/older age. Results for women and younger patients are less reliable.

4. **No clinical validation**: Any predictions from this project are for educational exploration only. They have not been clinically validated and should **never** be used for self-diagnosis or medical decision-making.

5. **Missing value imputation**: The 6 missing values in `ca` and `thal` will be imputed with median/mode. Imputation introduces a small amount of bias.
